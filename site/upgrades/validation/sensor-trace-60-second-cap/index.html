    Fixed 60-second trace cap

    Date: 2026-10-25. Applies to the current working tree on meshcore/sensor, based on 69891ef8 plus the existing recovery and acknowledgement changes. This supersedes the calculated multi-minute timeout in the earlier acknowledgement record.

    Behaviour and implementation

    Both !hillvue trace and !hillvue trace 7188 acknowledge an accepted start with:

    BME680 Sensor: Trace requested, please wait up to 60 seconds.

    The monotonic deadline is frozen at acceptance. TraceTransactionBudget now provides a fixed 60,000 ms duration and a 5,000 ms reporting reserve rather than summing maximum ten-hop phase allowances. The limit covers acknowledgement, discovery, queue waits, settling, probes, retries, grace and final reporting. Duplicate/start-busy/status requests and new routes cannot extend it.

    At actual TX start, existing airtime/hop receive allowances are shortened to share the remaining work time:

    Attempt Maximum share of remaining time before the 55-second cutoff
    Discovery 1 One quarter
    Discovery 2 One third
    Probe 1 One half
    Probe 2 All remaining work time

    The first discovery leaves room for a discovery retry and two probes. A quick successful discovery leaves more time for probing. Shorter existing phase allowances remain shorter. Queue delays are included because the share is calculated at actual TX start, using only timer arithmetic; observers do not query the transmitting radio. A retry is not admitted if its estimated TX watchdog plus requested delay cannot fit before the work cutoff.

    On the first running poll at 55 seconds, unfinished work ends. A previously stored valid fallback result can still be reported; otherwise the sensor queues Trace: discovery timed out or Trace: no result before timeout, according to the stage. A timeout generated at this cutoff has no application jitter. Its report budget is limited to the original deadline. A report already active is preserved and never replaced by a conflicting failure.

    At 60 seconds, owned queued packets are cancelled and the transaction releases its trace slot. Packets already on air remain exclusively under Dispatcher ownership until completion/watchdog recovery. Late results, discovery replies and callbacks do not reopen the expired transaction. Success can finish much earlier, and the existing node order, averaged SNR and final result format are preserved. Status includes the current phase and overall time remaining.

    The cap bounds application work on a running service loop, not successful RF receipt. Slow/congested links can time out despite being reachable. An in-flight frame cannot be recalled, and a blocked application cannot clean up until its next poll. Final multipart delivery is best effort within the same deadline; remaining unsent parts cannot continue past 60 seconds.

SHA256: a074c5cf5c6cd6f57221abc65fa83682211abb07e9cb493598abf09e7c37f4e5.

    Focused coverage includes:

    • Both commands acknowledge exactly 60 seconds, independent of route length, one/two-byte hashes, radio airtime and scoped/unscoped reply context.
    • Work and transaction boundaries at 55 and 60 seconds, adjacent milliseconds, rollover and delayed polls.
    • Discovery/probe receive-window clamping at TX start; shorter ordinary windows remain intact; slow-radio tracing can still use the second probe attempt.
    • A stage-specific discovery or probe timeout uses the final reporting window; a queued failure cannot acquire a fresh window after expiry.
    • Late probe replies during the report reserve cannot replace a timeout or restart work; late completion preserves Dispatcher ownership.
    • Cancellation, exhausted queues, allocation/queue/start failures, lost callbacks, status, duplicates, new trace after cleanup, and existing success/fallback/SNR and scope/history regressions.

    The older exact-phase tests continue to isolate acknowledgement through their explicit transport seam. Dedicated acknowledgement tests and the network model use the real Dispatcher and encryption. Native network tests model radio links and contention; they are not physical RF acceptance.

    Outstanding physical investigation

    No device has been flashed and no hardware acceptance has been performed. Check a successful and a silent target, bare and targeted commands, busy/slow radio, cancellation, 55/60-second cleanup and a subsequent request without reboot.

    This change does not fix or establish the cause of the sensor discovery failure for 7188. Zen succeeding from the same location does not establish sensor identity/ACL eligibility, scope equivalence or acceptance of returning replies. That read-only comparison and packet-level diagnosis remain a separate investigation. The 60-second limit shortens waiting and guarantees bounded software ownership; it does not claim that the destination is unreachable.
