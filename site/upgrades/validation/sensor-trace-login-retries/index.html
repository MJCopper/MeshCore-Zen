    Two guest-login retries — 25 October 2026

    Guest login now allows an initial attempt and two retries: at most three login transmissions per targeted trace. An unanswered reply window or failed TX can advance to the next attempt after bounded backoff (250 then 500 ms). Every submitted login uses a fresh timestamp from the existing durable clock. Local allocation/admission retries remain within the current queue lease.

    An accepted reply stops further logins and enables the second ordinary path-discovery request. Invalid/unexpected roles, clock failure, queue expiry, cancellation, exhausted attempts and the control cutoff do not generate more logins. Protected guest access is tested through all three attempts and still permits successful advert fallback. Cancellation and stale callbacks retain existing ownership protections.

    Each attempt retains a separate eight-second queue allowance, airtime-based TX watchdog and up to eight seconds for the acknowledgement after successful local TX completion. The control cutoff moves from 25 to 40 seconds from acceptance to accommodate three reply windows plus ordinary discovery. All phases are clipped to that cutoff; arbitrary queue delays can prevent all attempts fitting. The original 55-second work and 60-second overall deadlines remain. This leaves 15 seconds after the latest control cutoff for probe work and five for final reporting; long routes are not guaranteed to complete within the hard cap.

    At most ten application-originated packets can emit: two discovery requests, three logins, two TRACE probes, one acknowledgement and two report parts. Diagnostics preserve slots 0/1/2 (initial discovery, first login, final discovery) and add slots 3/4 for the two login retries. Status includes login attempt N/3.

    Path persistence is unchanged: successful trace results update RAM-only route history (15-minute TTL). A newly discovered path is not inserted into the persistent advert-route cache. An already matching advert hint is marked as validated in RAM; that outcome alone does not schedule a flash checkpoint. Advert-derived hints persist via the existing deferred advert-cache saves.

    Four new regressions cover exactly three attempts with independent receive windows, stopping on second-attempt success, absolute cutoff/cancellation, and three real Dispatcher transmissions using distinct durable timestamps. Existing single-login timeout tests now expect the bounded retry state. Protected-target integration expects three login requests. Busy/status tests explicitly advance polls or the updated control deadline rather than relying on the old cutoff.
